  <p><strong>BrainConnects</strong> is a grounds-up academic network and meeting series in neuroimaging and cognitive neuroscience, with activities centred on Southeast Asia and the wider Asia-Pacific region. Established in 2014, BrainConnects brings together researchers, clinicians, engineers, educators, and trainees to exchange methods, build collaborations, and strengthen regional capacity in neuroimaging sciences.</p>

  <p>The network is self-organised and sustained through local hosts, collaborators, and in-kind support from participating institutions and countries. Its mission places particular emphasis on supporting early career investigators by creating opportunities for presentation, training, cross-country mentorship, and access to regional scientific networks.</p>

  <h2>Distinction from NIH BRAIN CONNECTS</h2>
  <p>BrainConnects is distinct from the <strong>BRAIN Initiative Connectivity across Scales (BRAIN CONNECTS)</strong> programme of the United States National Institutes of Health. The NIH programme was launched through 2022 BRAIN Initiative funding opportunities to support large-scale technologies, datasets, and infrastructure for mapping neural connectivity across mammalian brains.</p>

  <p>By contrast, BrainConnects began in 2014 as a regional, investigator-led collaboration in Asia-Pacific neuroimaging and ageing neuroscience. It is not an NIH funding programme or part of the NIH BRAIN CONNECTS Network. The similarity in names reflects overlapping scientific interest in brain connectivity, but the organisations, origins, governance, and activities are separate.</p>

  <h2>History</h2>
  <p>BrainConnects emerged from collaborations between the Clinical Brain Lab at Nanyang Technological University (NTU), Singapore, and the NeuroImaging and Informatics Lab at the National Center for Geriatrics and Gerontology (NCGG), Japan. Early activities were supported by science and technology cooperation between Singapore and Japan, with funding support from the Japan Society for the Promotion of Science (JSPS), NTU, and the National University of Singapore (NUS) in 2014 and 2015.</p>

  <p>The initial collaboration also involved the Cognitive Engineering Lab at the Singapore Institute for Neurotechnology (SINAPSE), NUS, and the Brain and Mind Research Center at Nagoya University. It subsequently expanded to include partners in Taiwan and Malaysia, including National Tsing Hua University in 2016 and Universiti Teknologi PETRONAS in 2017.</p>

  <p>Early BrainConnects meetings focused on the application of neuroimaging to ageing, cognitive and physical interventions, mobile brain and body imaging, neuro-robotics, and interdisciplinary approaches to neuroinformatics. These activities grew from earlier international collaborations in medical informatics and helped define BrainConnects as a regional forum for ageing neuroinformatics and applied neuroimaging research.</p>

  <h2>Mission and Activities</h2>
  <p>BrainConnects aims to build research capacity in neuroimaging sciences across Southeast Asia and the Asia-Pacific. Its activities support knowledge exchange, training, and collaboration among laboratories and institutions with different levels of infrastructure and access to neuroimaging resources.</p>

  <p>The network emphasises practical support for early career investigators, including opportunities to present work, receive feedback from senior researchers, participate in workshops, and form cross-border collaborations. Meeting themes have covered ageing neuroscience, neurodegenerative disorders, brain-computer interfaces, neuroimaging methods, artificial intelligence, learning sciences, neurotechnology, and interdisciplinary approaches to brain health.</p>
